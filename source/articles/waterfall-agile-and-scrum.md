# Waterfall, Agile and Scrum

**Covers:** Waterfall, Agile, Scrum, Product Owner, Scrum Master

## Three ways of organizing the same work {#overview}
Teams build software in very different ways. At one end is a plan made in full at the start and followed step by step. At the other is a loop of short cycles in which the plan changes as the team learns. Waterfall, Agile and Scrum sit along this range. Knowing what each really means helps you choose, and helps you avoid the common confusion between a mindset and a method.

## Waterfall {#waterfall}
Waterfall is an approach that moves through fixed phases in strict order, such as planning, then building, then testing, with each phase finished before the next begins. Like water down steps, it does not go back up easily.

It works well when requirements are stable and well understood, for example when building to a legal standard, or when changes are very expensive, as in hardware. It struggles when you learn something in the middle that should change the plan, because late changes mean redoing finished work. Customers also see nothing until the end.

## Agile {#agile}
Agile is a mindset, not one fixed process. It values responding to change and delivering working software over following an upfront plan to the letter. The Agile Manifesto of 2001 states four values: individuals and interactions over processes and tools, working software over comprehensive documentation, customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to change over following a plan. The things on the right still matter, but the things on the left matter more.

Agile is behind many methods, including Scrum and flow-based boards. A team can use Agile practices and still be unorganized, so the mindset needs discipline to work.

## Scrum {#scrum}
Scrum is a specific Agile framework built around fixed-length cycles called sprints, a few defined roles and a small set of regular meetings. A team commits to a chunk of work for each sprint, delivers something usable, reviews it with stakeholders and reflects on how to improve.

Scrum is deliberately light. It tells you the roles, the meetings and the lists of work, and it leaves the technical practices to the team.

## Product Owner {#product-owner}
The Product Owner is the Scrum role that owns the list of work to be done and decides what the team should build next, based on what matters most to users and the business. They are the single voice that sets priorities, which stops a team from being pulled in several directions.

A good Product Owner talks to users and stakeholders often, is available to answer the team's questions and is willing to say no.

## Scrum Master {#scrum-master}
The Scrum Master is the role that helps the team follow the process and work well. They run meetings when needed, remove blockers, protect the team from outside interruptions and coach people. They are not the team's boss. A Scrum Master does not assign the tasks or approve the finished work.

## A worked example {#example}
A company wants to build an expense app. Compare two ways of running it.

| | Waterfall | Scrum |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Full plan for all 24 weeks, written first | Rough goal; plan the next 2 weeks only |
| First visible result | Week 20 | End of week 2 |
| User feedback | At the end | After each 2-week sprint (12 chances) |
| A change in week 9 | Formal change process, rework | Product Owner reorders the list for the next sprint |
| Who sets priorities | The plan | The Product Owner |
| Who removes blockers | The project manager | The Scrum Master |

Count the feedback chances: 24 weeks divided by 2-week sprints gives 12. If users reveal in week 9 that the receipt scan matters more than the report screen, the Scrum team can move it up immediately. The Waterfall team would have to run a change process, and the work already done on reports may be wasted.

Neither is always better. If the requirements were fixed by regulation, the waterfall plan could be the right one.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Calling a team "Agile" because it holds daily meetings, while still working to a fixed plan that it cannot change. Another mistake is treating the Scrum Master as a manager who assigns work. The role serves the team. A third is a Product Owner who is too busy to decide, which leaves the team guessing.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Waterfall runs phases in strict order and fits stable requirements.
- Agile is a mindset that values working software and responding to change.
- Scrum is one Agile framework, with sprints, roles and regular meetings.
- The Product Owner sets priorities. The Scrum Master helps the team work well.
