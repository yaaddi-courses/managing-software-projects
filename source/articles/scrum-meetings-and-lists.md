# The Scrum rhythm: sprints, meetings and the backlog

**Covers:** Sprint, Sprint planning, Sprint Review, Daily standup, Backlog, Retrospective

## A repeating beat {#overview}
Scrum teams work to a steady rhythm. The same cycle repeats, each with its own meetings, so that planning, delivering and improving become habits. The rhythm is useful because it makes progress visible, gives regular chances to correct course and keeps meetings short and purposeful. This article walks through one cycle in order.

## Backlog {#backlog}
The backlog is the full ordered list of work the team could do, with the most important items at the top. It holds features, fixes and improvements that are not yet started. It is never "finished", because new ideas keep arriving. The Product Owner owns the order, and the team reads from the top when it plans.

Items near the top should be clear and small enough to start. Items further down can be vague, because they may never be done or may change.

## Sprint {#sprint}
A sprint is a short, fixed-length period, commonly one to four weeks, during which the team commits to a set of work and delivers something usable. The length stays the same from sprint to sprint, which builds a predictable rhythm. During a sprint, the goal is protected: new requests wait for the next one unless they are urgent.

## Sprint planning {#sprint-planning}
Sprint planning is the meeting at the start of a sprint where the team decides what to commit to. It looks at the top of the backlog, the team's actual capacity (after holidays and other duties) and the goal for the sprint. The team chooses what it believes it can finish, then breaks the chosen items into tasks.

The commitment should be realistic. Taking on too much leads to rushed work and unfinished items.

## Daily standup {#daily-standup}
The daily standup is a short meeting, about 15 minutes, held every day at the same time. Each person says what they did yesterday, what they plan today and what is blocking them. It is for the team to coordinate with each other, not a progress report for a manager. Longer discussions are moved to a separate conversation afterward.

## Sprint Review {#sprint-review}
The Sprint Review is the meeting at the end of the sprint where the team shows the work it finished to stakeholders and collects feedback. It is a demonstration of working software, not a slide presentation. The feedback goes back into the backlog and may change the priorities.

## Retrospective {#retrospective}
A retrospective is a meeting where the team honestly looks at how it worked and decides what to keep and what to change. It follows the review at the end of the sprint. A good one produces one or two specific actions with owners, such as "ask for design files before the sprint starts". It focuses on the process and not on blaming people.

## A worked example {#example}
A team has a two-week sprint with 5 people, 10 working days and 2 people-days lost to holidays.

1. **Capacity.** 5 people times 10 days is 50 person-days. Minus 2 holiday days gives 48 person-days.
2. **Sprint planning (Monday of week 1).** The team pulls the top backlog items that sum to about 45 person-days, leaving a margin. It sets a goal: "Customers can reset their password".
3. **Daily standups (every morning).** 15 minutes each. On day 4 a developer says "I am waiting for the email template", and the Scrum Master gets it that afternoon.
4. **Sprint Review (Friday of week 2).** The team shows the new reset flow to two stakeholders. One asks for a clearer error message. It goes into the backlog.
5. **Retrospective (right after).** The team notices that two items were late because requirements were unclear. Action: refine the next items before planning.

Per person, the meetings take about 2.5 hours of standups (15 minutes times 10 days) plus an hour each for the review and the retrospective. That is about 4.5 hours out of 80 working hours, or roughly 6 percent, before counting planning.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Letting the standup become a progress report to the manager, or a long discussion. People stop listening. Another mistake is skipping the retrospective when the team is busy. That is when problems repeat. A third is changing the sprint goal in the middle of the sprint, which destroys the team's focus.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The backlog is the ordered list of future work, and the sprint is a fixed-length cycle.
- Sprint planning picks work to fit capacity, the standup coordinates daily and the review shows results.
- The retrospective produces a few concrete improvements.
- The meetings take little time and give regular chances to correct course.
